Every page has a Feedback on this page link at the bottom. What you send goes to the CPUC's CEDARS team with the page you came from, so nobody has to ask where you were. When a request takes a fix or an improvement, the team joins it to a work item, and the work items are built in the order of the backlog.

Sending a request

  1. On the page it is about, choose Feedback on this page. Say whether it is a question, a problem, an accessibility barrier, an idea, a comment or a security concern, and write your message. Leave out customers' names, addresses and account numbers.
  2. Before a question is sent, the form lists the parts of Help that share words with it, in case one answers it. Nothing is sent until you choose Send.
  3. Signed in, you follow it on My requests (also at the bottom of every page). Signed out, it is sent without your name and cannot be answered.
  • Received means the team has it, Being looked at that someone is working on it, Waiting for you that the team asked you a question, Answered that they replied, and Closed that it is finished. A question for you waits in My queue too, and you are told of each answer on your Notifications page and by email unless you turned that off.
  • Reply on the request's page while it is open. When you have what you need, say it is settled and it closes.
  • Accessibility barriers and security concerns are urgent. A security concern is read only by the CPUC's system administrators.
  • When you act for an organization, its administrators can read your questions, problems and accessibility barriers so they can help, unless you tick the box to keep one from them. They never read ideas, comments or security concerns.
  • When your request is joined to a work item, its page says where that work stands: being considered, to be done, planned for a release, or not planned, with the reason.
  • Known problems and Upcoming changes list the work items the CPUC made public. Signed in, you can say a known problem affects you too.

How the support desk answers

The CPUC's support desk (the "Support: answer requests" permission) and its system administrators answer requests on {% page_link 'feedback:inbox' "Feedback" %}. Requests waiting on the CPUC are also in their My queue, urgent ones first.

  • Mark a request as being looked at, then answer it or ask the sender a question. A note is for staff only. Close it with a note on what was done, or reopen a closed one with a reason. Every step is in the audit log.
  • When answering it takes a fix or an improvement, join the request to a work item that already covers it, or propose a new one in your own words. A request is joined to one work item at a time.

Work items and the backlog

A work item is one problem to fix or one improvement to make, written as a story: who needs it, on which pages, what and why, with a size, a severity for a problem, a workaround and acceptance tests that say what done means. The requests joined to it are its evidence. {% page_link 'work:backlog' "Work items" %} holds them.

  • Backlog managers ("Work items: manage the backlog") accept a proposed item into the backlog or propose declining it, put the backlog in order (what is first is built first), propose making an item public, and fill release plans from the top of the backlog.
  • CPUC approvers ("Work items: approve") approve or turn down what a backlog manager proposes: declining an item, making one public, and a release plan. When every item of an approved plan is built, the CPUC decides whether the release goes; its items are then Done and the people waiting on them are told.
  • The senders of the requests joined to an item are told when a release plan that holds it is approved and when it is declined.

Rounds of testing

On the sites where testing is switched on (the sandbox and test sites), the CPUC tests a release from versioned test scripts.

  • A test lead (a CPUC system administrator) opens a round with a version of the scripts and its dates on the Testing page, assigns each script to testers' own accounts, follows their progress, reads or downloads the results, and closes the round.
  • A tester finds the scripts assigned to them on My testing and in My queue, steps through each test (which account to use, what must be true first, the steps and the result to expect) and records what they found. A failed or blocked result is also sent to Feedback as a finding, and answered like any other request.
  • A work item's acceptance test may name a test of the scripts; that test is then run in the release's round, and its results show on the release's page.
