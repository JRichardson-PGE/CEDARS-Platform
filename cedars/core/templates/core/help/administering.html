Administration is shared. Each organization's own administrators manage its members and their permissions; the CPUC assigns only an organization's first administrator. CPUC system administrators keep the organizations, role templates, access settings, retention and the system itself. Every change is recorded in the audit log, with who made it, for which organization, why, and the record before and after.

An organization's administrators

  1. Open your organization from Organizations. Its page lists its codes, members and their permissions, access requests waiting for a decision, its yearly recertification and its members' API tokens.
  2. Approve or decline each access request. A request for personal information (PII) also needs a CPUC data steward's approval after yours.
  3. Grant a member a single permission, or a role template (such as Evaluator or Consultant) to give every permission of that role at once. Revoke a permission, or remove a member, when it is no longer needed.
  4. When a recertification opens, certify each grant to keep it or remove it by the due date. A grant not certified in time may be suspended, as the access settings say; Reinstate restores it.

CPUC system administrators

  • Organizations: add an organization by its CEDARS code and name, assign its first administrator, change its name or PA type, rename its code (the history is kept) or merge in an organization recorded under a former code.
  • Role templates: named bundles of module permissions that organization administrators grant. A template never holds an organization's administrator, PII access or the CPUC's own roles: each needs a decision of its own.
  • Access settings: how often grants are recertified, whether grants not certified are suspended, and whether PII is only for federated organizations and sessions.
  • Data stewardship: CPUC data stewards approve PII access, and a change to the PII rules takes one steward to propose and a second to confirm.
  • Filing calendar: change a deadline's due date, opening date or notes on its page, with a reason; those who follow the calendar are told.
  • CPUC goal decisions: load each CPUC decision's goals from the templates; a second administrator confirms them.
  • Publication holds and the scan settings: what the scan for personal information looks for before a filing reaches the public data.
  • Document accessibility: what the check on each stored PDF and workbook found, and whether it only warns or refuses an upload.
  • Retention: how long each class of record is kept. Nothing under a legal hold is deleted.
  • Feedback: every message sent from a page's Feedback link; close each with a note on what was done.
  • Jobs and health: every health check, failing first; every system administrator is told when one starts failing. Engine releases is where the site moves to a new release of the calculation engine.
