The filing cycle

Budget filings follow the four-year program cycle: a business plan application, then true-up (TUAL) and mid-cycle (MCAL) advice letters. Budgets lists each cycle's filings with their due dates; the newest cycle is open and earlier ones are folded under their heading. The filing calendar has every due date.

Filing a budget

  1. Upload the filing: one .zip with Measure.csv and ProgramCost.csv (the way CET takes a job) or one Excel workbook with Measure and ProgramCost sheets. Field names may be CEDARS' or CET's.
  2. Check: CEDARS checks every record against the budget filing rules. The version's page lists the findings by rule, rejects first, and the findings file names each line, record, field and rule and says how to fix it. Fix and upload again; the new upload replaces the version's earlier one.
  3. Price: once nothing is rejected, CEDARS runs the cost-effectiveness calculation on the cycle's ACC and allocation version. The run's page shows its progress.
  4. Confirm: the priced results are what confirming signs off on. A confirmed filing is published.

Before the due date you can un-confirm (the version goes back to validated and is unpublished). After it, a correction is a new version with a reason: Ask to reopen, and the earlier version stays as filed. Every confirmed version is kept, and Compare two versions shows what changed.

A program's budget is its ProgramCost row plus its measures' rebates and incentives, by program year.

Fund shifts and the operating budget

A fund shift moves budget between programs within the cycle. Name it and say why, enter the lines (they must add up to zero), then submit. A shift touching a statewide program changes each co-funding PA's share too: each co-funder acknowledges it and says where its part comes from, and the shift takes effect once all have. The operating budget is the standing filing plus the shifts in effect; the dashboard's Budget and spending page compares spending with it. A later filing is taken to carry the earlier shifts, and its page says so.
